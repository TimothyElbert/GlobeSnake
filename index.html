Globe Snake

The old game — eat, grow, never touch yourself — played on the surface of the real Earth. The food is places. Brazil is easy. Paris is harder. Big Ben is harder still, and somewhere past that lies Point Nemo, the furthest spot on the planet from anywhere at all. Find them fast, find them without help, and the points come. Mountains slow you down. Rivers carry you. Your own tail is getting longer.

Terra Incognita

A blank vellum globe and a pen for a snake. Nothing is drawn until you go there — not even the coastlines — so finding your bearings is the game rather than a preliminary to it. There is no pin hint here either. Only a bearing, a distance, and your own nerve.

How it plays

  • Move the mouse — the snake chases your cursor, and how far ahead you reach is your throttle. Hold to boost. That is the whole control scheme.
  • Space buys a hint against the current target's value. It never touches points you have already banked — and it points you at a region, never straight at the answer.
  • A target is worth less the longer it takes, down to nothing. You still have to find it to move on.
  • Stuck for twice the par time and the first hint arrives free. You cannot get permanently lost.
  • Every run ends with a route card: your whole journey, coloured by the climates you crossed.
